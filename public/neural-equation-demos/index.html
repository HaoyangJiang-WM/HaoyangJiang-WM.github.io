03 / MODELING & NUMERICS

Beyond a model name.

Choose the state, specify the equation, solve it consistently, and differentiate the same numerical problem.

01

Neural ODE

ż = fθ(z, u)

Learn a vector field, then integrate from the given state. Best suited here when the observed variables already form a closed state.

RK4 · discrete adjoints · end-to-end trajectory loss

02

Neural DDE

ẏ = Fθ(y(t), y(t − τ), u)

Learn both the response and delay locations. Queries after time zero read the model’s own predicted history, never future labels.

Learned delays · differentiable history interpolation

03

Attention NIE

y = h(0) + Aθ[y] − Aθ[h](0)

A learned causal integral interaction acts on observed history and unknown future states. The equation is solved self-consistently.

Lag-aware kernel · implicit differentiation · solver audits

RESEARCH TAKEAWAY

Memory matters.
Its representation matters just as much.

The delay model outperformed the tested history-encoded NODEs and attention NIE on these two memory tasks. A compact current-state NODE was stronger on the closed-state control. The useful result is the distinction—not a claim that one neural equation is universally superior.

Metric: 100 × RMSE after each state is normalized by its training standard deviation. This is not raw relative L2. A failed solve is kept as “Failed,” not omitted. Mackey–Glass ANIE failures use the original 35-Newton-iteration protocol.

What was held fixed?

128 training histories, 32 validation histories, 0.5% observation noise, exact current state, and the same known controls. Every neural weight was trained jointly with Adam and L-BFGS; learned delays received gradients too. Checkpoints were chosen on validation data before the new test inputs were generated.

What are the limitations?

These are compact, self-implemented research prototypes, not official ANIE or Latent ODE reproductions. The thermal task predicts two sensors in a fixed 1D simulation. The strongest baseline is not best on every frequency shift. Delays are effective learned parameters, not guaranteed physical travel times. No universal superiority or state-of-the-art claim is made.

What does the implementation demonstrate?

Continuous-time modeling; differentiable interpolation and delay parameters; discrete and implicit adjoints; validation-selected training; forward and gradient checks; explicit failure reporting; long-horizon and frequency-shift evaluation. The browser replays archived predictions and does not perform training.
